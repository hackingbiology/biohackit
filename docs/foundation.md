# biohack.it & Hacking Biology Foundation — strategic evaluation

*Working evaluation of the relationship between the software/initiative (**biohack.it**) and the non-profit steward (**Hacking Biology Foundation**). Feeds the About section and the future hackingbiology.com. English; principle-level, no economic figures.*

---

## 1. Why two names, one mission

- **biohack.it** — the **software and the initiative**: the open platform the community uses to document protocols and measure them.
- **Hacking Biology Foundation** — the **non-profit that stewards it**: it manages the project and its development, and it exists to *guarantee* the properties the community must be able to trust.

The separation is not cosmetic. A tool can be forked, sold, or captured; a **steward with a mission lock** is what makes a credible, permanent promise. The Foundation is the guarantor; biohack.it is the thing guaranteed.

## 2. Incorporation & ownership

- The Foundation is to be **incorporated in Roatán (Honduras), within the Próspera ZEDE** — a special economic zone with regulatory autonomy — deliberately, **to preserve a progressive spirit of experimentation** in longevity and biohacking, alongside an aligned community.
- **Ownership.** The Foundation **owns the BIOHACK.IT software and its brand, trademark and copyright**. It cannot relicense the software away from **AGPL-3.0** or from the mission.
- *Note — incorporation ≠ data hosting.* Where the entity is incorporated is separate from where user data lives. For users in regulated jurisdictions (e.g. the EU), data-protection duties are met at the **hosting/technical layer**, independently of the Próspera seat.

## 3. What the Foundation guarantees (the mission lock)

1. **Never commercial.** The project is never sold, taken proprietary, or turned into a walled garden. This is the reason a Foundation, not a company, holds it.
2. **Open by code and by data.** Software under **AGPL-3.0**; the public dataset released as a clonable snapshot; content under open licenses. Anyone can run their own instance.
3. **Harm reduction first.** Safety guardrails are non-optional; the platform *describes, never prescribes*.
4. **Transparency of method.** Where AI is and is not used is published (the AI surfaces map); every reused upstream is attributed and notified — the project is a *connector of others' data effort*, not a silent consumer.
5. **Data belongs to the person.** Full export always; the individual, not the platform, owns the record.

## 4. Governance

- The Foundation **holds the stewardship**: the domain, the trademark, the software copyright, the canonical instance, and the guarantee of the above. It cannot relicense the software away from AGPL or the mission.
- **The board is a basket of biohackers and software hackers** — practitioners of the domain and builders of the tool — chosen so the project stays **impartial** and **on-mission over time**. Mixing the two keeps neither capture nor drift easy: the biohackers hold it to the community's needs, the hackers hold it to open, verifiable engineering.
- **Development** is directed by the Foundation with an open contributor community; ADRs and specs are public from day one (this repository).
- **Community governance** attaches to the existing community (Rapamycin News), not a cold-launched forum. Contested claims and study proposals are public and discussed, not privately triaged.

## 5. Sustainability

- The Foundation is funded **only by public grants**.
- Beyond grants, **equity participations in dedicated startups** that pursue the *same* goal may arise — aligned ventures whose success feeds the mission, never the reverse.
- The knowledge is never for sale: protocols, biomarkers, safety rules and the open dataset are not products; nothing lets a supplier buy influence over which protocols emerge.

## 6. Public presence — two sites, two jobs

- **biohack.it** — the **product/initiative** site (live): what it is, the plan, join the community. Speaks to biohackers and the community.
- **hackingbiology.com** — the **organisation** site (to be rebuilt from Fabio's blog): who the Foundation is, the mission lock, governance, how to support or partner. Speaks to institutions, researchers, funders, and the sceptical clinician.

The About section of biohack.it links to hackingbiology.com; the two reinforce one message — *open infrastructure for longevity, stewarded so it stays that way.*

## 7. Risks & mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Mission drift over time | Foundation charter + AGPL + open-data licenses that cannot be quietly reversed; mixed biohacker/hacker board |
| Funding pressure toward commercialisation | Public-grant funding; only mission-aligned startup equity; data and knowledge never sold |
| Capture of the canonical instance | Full clonability + self-hosting: the community can always run its own |
| Regulatory qualification as a medical device | Descriptive-not-prescriptive posture by design; legal review before public launch |
| Jurisdiction vs data protection | Próspera incorporation for the entity; EU/user data protection handled at the hosting layer |
