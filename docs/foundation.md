# biohack.it & Hacking Biology Foundation — strategic evaluation

*Working evaluation of the relationship between the software/initiative (**biohack.it**) and the non-profit steward (**Hacking Biology Foundation**). Feeds the About section and the future hackingbiology.com. English; principle-level, no economic figures.*

---

## 1. Why two names, one mission

- **biohack.it** — the **software and the initiative**: the open platform the community uses to document protocols and measure them.
- **Hacking Biology Foundation** — the **non-profit that stewards it**: it manages the project and its development, and it exists to *guarantee* the properties the community must be able to trust.

The separation is not cosmetic. A tool can be forked, sold, or captured; a **steward with a mission lock** is what makes a credible, permanent promise. The Foundation is the guarantor; biohack.it is the thing guaranteed.

## 2. What the Foundation guarantees (the mission lock)

These are the commitments the Foundation exists to make binding:

1. **Never commercial.** The project will not be sold, taken proprietary, or turned into a walled garden. This is the reason a Foundation, not a company, holds it.
2. **Open by code and by data.** Software under **AGPL-3.0**; the public dataset released as a clonable snapshot; content under open licenses (CC). Anyone can run their own instance.
3. **Harm reduction first.** Safety guardrails are non-optional; the platform *describes, never prescribes*.
4. **No conflict of interest.** The Foundation does not sell the tests it suggests, does not monetise procurement, and forbids paid placement in the protocol catalogue. Market players may **integrate**, never **classify**.
5. **Transparency of method.** Where AI is and is not used is published (the AI surfaces map); every reused upstream is attributed and notified — the project is a *connector of others' data effort*, not a silent consumer.
6. **Data belongs to the person.** Full export always; the individual, not the platform, owns the record.

## 3. Governance (proposed shape)

- The **Foundation holds the stewardship**: the domain, the trademarks, the canonical instance, and the guarantee of the above. It cannot relicense the software away from AGPL or the mission.
- **Development** is directed by the Foundation with an open contributor community; ADRs and specs public from day one (this repository).
- **Community governance** attaches to the existing community (Rapamycin News), not a cold-launched forum. Contested claims and study proposals are public and discussed (not privately triaged).
- A **conflict-of-interest charter** is a founding document, not an afterthought — it is the single most important thing to get right for credibility with medicine and research.

## 4. Sustainability without compromise

The economic cycle is **subordinate to the mission** and must never touch the two things that would poison trust: the data and the procurement.

- **Acceptable:** grants and donations; institutional/research partnerships; commissions on *people's time* (e.g. verified-creator coaching and editorial content) where the platform takes a cut of a service, never of the knowledge.
- **Excluded, by charter:** selling protocols, biomarkers, safety rules, or the open dataset; paid ranking; affiliate-driven procurement; anything that makes a supplier able to influence which protocols emerge.

> This keeps the promise legible: *you can pay a person for their time; you can never buy the knowledge or the ranking.*

## 5. Public presence — two sites, two jobs

- **biohack.it** — the **product/initiative** site (live): what it is, the plan, join the community. Speaks to biohackers and the community.
- **hackingbiology.com** — the **organisation** site (to be rebuilt from Fabio's blog): who the Foundation is, the mission lock, governance, the conflict-of-interest charter, how to support or partner. Speaks to institutions, researchers, funders, and the sceptical clinician.

The About section of biohack.it links to hackingbiology.com; the two reinforce one message — *open infrastructure for longevity, stewarded so it stays that way.*

## 6. Risks & mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Mission drift over time | Foundation charter + AGPL + open-data licenses that cannot be quietly reversed |
| Funding pressure toward commercialisation | Sustainability limited to service/time revenue; data and procurement ring-fenced by charter |
| Conflict of interest with suppliers/labs | Integrate-not-classify rule; no monetised procurement; mandatory disclosure |
| Regulatory qualification as a medical device | Descriptive-not-prescriptive posture by design; legal review before public launch |
| Capture of the canonical instance | Full clonability + self-hosting: the community can always run its own |
