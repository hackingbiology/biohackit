The gap

Longevity intervention is running far ahead of longevity measurement.

People already adopt off-label drugs, peptides, HBOT, plasmapheresis. But the protocol lives in spreadsheets, forum posts and chat prompts — not comparable, not followable, not readable by a physician.

Bryan Johnson has a lab and a team. Everyone else has a spreadsheet. A thousand N-of-1 on the same protocol, made comparable, are a different scientific object entirely — and it doesn’t exist yet.

The mechanism

Follow and copy the people who get results — and see their biomarkers.

The social dynamic that powers copy-trading in finance, applied to health. The value is not the single datapoint; it is the ability to follow, compare and copy, anchored to structured, public biomarkers rather than anecdote.

  • A protocol becomes something you can subscribe to — and it carries its safety markers with it.
  • Every compound, every intervention gets a thread anchored to real, comparable data.
  • A living community is the only quality control that actually works on self-reported data.

Document your protocol. Measure it. Share it.

Protocol and results, published per-marker with explicit consent. Anonymised, standards-coded, released as open data.

Design principles

Rigor is a design decision, not a disclaimer.

  • Describes, never prescribes. Suggests what to measure, never what to take or how much.
  • Every claim is graded to its evidence. Human clinical → observational → animal → in vitro → anecdotal.
  • The LLM writes words, never numbers. Extraction, mapping and thresholds are deterministic and reproducible.
  • It fails loudly. Never drops an item silently — it shows the diff between input and output.
  • It stops instead of guessing. When data is insufficient, it says so — no approximate index.
  • Some data is structurally non-shareable. For genomics there is simply no code path that publishes it.

Data model

Three questions, deliberately kept apart.

TestingProtocol

What I measure, and when. A versioned set of measures and timepoints.

TreatmentPlan

What I take and do. Versioned interventions — self-managed or physician-assigned.

Goal

What I want to change. Mapped onto the Hallmarks of Aging.

Substance→ SafetyRule→ Biomarker→ Measurement→ Dashboard

“What you take” and “how you are” are the same data structure read from two sides. That single relation is also the answer to the two questions a doctor asks.

Harm reduction

Follow a protocol — inherit its safety.

  • Mandatory baseline before you can follow a protocol at all.
  • Safety markers inherited automatically from every active compound — safety is never opt-in.
  • Dose sanity check catches the extra zero — plausibility validation of a number, not clinical advice.
  • Overdue is a safety gap, not a calendar reminder — “rapamycin active for 94 days, liver panel overdue by 34.”

This is the module that justifies the project. Biohackers already take the risk today — without any of it.

A hard boundary in the pipeline

Extraction, mapping and threshold calculation are deterministic and reproducible. The LLM only produces the plain-language narration — generated once and cached. The words, never the numbers.

Built for aggregation from day one

LOINC + UCUM + method saved with every single measurement — the only architecture compatible with releasing open data. Three ranges per analyte: laboratory reference, longevity-optimal, safety.

A sentence you can say on television without pronouncing the word “biohacking”, with no regulatory exposure — and it works with a single user and no community.

Built for hackers

You own the data

Full export, always, no friction. Public is per-marker and opt-in. Delete means delete. No account required to start.

Local-first option

Run the individual layer entirely in your browser — contributing to a cohort is a separate, explicit choice, never a default.

Agent Access · MCP

A revocable, read-only token so you query your own biomarkers with your own agent — Claude, Cursor, a Nostr bot. No competitor will ever ship this.

Walled gardens are impossible here by construction: AGPL means anyone who runs a modified version as a service must open the source. Don’t like a decision? Fork it.

Build nothing that already exists

Standards, not silos.

Speak the standards

HL7 FHIR internally; OMOP CDM export for research — so what you produce is real research data with proper coding, not another wellness silo.

Component reuse

The biomarker catalogue, unit conversions and import pipeline of get-based — same AGPL licence — vendored, not forked, with a LOINC layer contributed upstream.

Existing community

The forum is not rebuilt: Rapamycin News is already there. Link first, cohorts second — never the reverse.

Open data released only with cohort thresholds and declared provenance. A small clean dataset beats a large uncomparable one — that is the difference between being cited and being ignored.
